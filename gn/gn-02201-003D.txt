Effective Dates: 10/13/2023 - Present | TN 72 (06-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0202201003 (fetched 2026-10-04)

D. Retroactive entitlement causes an overpayment for those individuals in a different
household
When we retroactively entitle someone on the same record and they live in a different
household, we might have to adjust the benefit rates for everyone receiving benefits
on the same record. For individuals living in a different household from the late-filing
beneficiary, we apply normal overpayment recovery action against the individuals who
were already receiving benefits. For information regarding when 202(j)(1) applies,
see RS 00615.760.
EXAMPLE: In July 2009, Lola and Felix are receiving child’s benefits on their parent, James’,
record. James has another child, Oliver, who was born in April 2009, lives in a different
household, and becomes retroactively entitled to benefits on James’ record effective
May 2009. Because of Oliver’s entitlement, we must adjust Lola and Felix’s benefit
amount beginning May 2009. We overpaid Lola and Felix for May 2009 through June 2009
while we underpaid Oliver for the same period. Because Oliver lives in a different
household, we will release the underpayment to Oliver and not use their underpayment
to recover Lola and Felix’s overpayment. We will post the overpayment to Lola and
Felix and follow normal overpayment procedures.
