GN 03316.090 Disclosure Without Consent to Social Security Agencies of Foreign Countries that are
Parties to Totalization Agreements
Section 233 of the Social Security Act (Act) (42 U.S.C. § 433) authorizes the President of the
United States to enter into totalization agreements with other countries to coordinate
the Social Security systems of both countries. These agreements help fill gaps in
benefit protection for workers who divide their careers between the United States
and another country. Such workers may fail to qualify for Social Security benefits
from the United States or the other country because they have not worked long enough
to meet minimum eligibility requirements. Under these agreements, each country can
count credits earned under the other country’s system, if this will help the worker
meet the minimum length-of-work requirements to qualify. The agreements also provide
that each country will assist the other in implementing and administering the agreement.
