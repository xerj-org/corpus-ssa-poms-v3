A. Procedure for handling adverse claims
An adverse claim is an award of benefits to a subsequent claimant or the reinstatement or resumption
of benefits to a prior beneficiary that results in the non-entitlement or termination of an earlier beneficiary
1. Identifying adverse claims
In most cases, you learn of a possible adverse claim by reviewing the Master Beneficiary
Record (MBR) or Inquiry Response (I/R) in Modernized Claims System (MCS) screens after
receiving a subsequent claim.
2. Example of an adverse claim
Liz files for benefits for their child and themselves (subsequent claims) on the social
security number (SSN) of their disabled spouse. The subsequent claimants live apart
from the number holder (NH) and the parent alleges that they never divorced the NH.
However, the MBR shows current entitlement of a common-law spouse and stepchildren
living with the NH. In this case, Liz and the child’s claim are adverse claims because
their award to benefits will result in the non-entitlement of the common-law spouse
and the stepchildren.
