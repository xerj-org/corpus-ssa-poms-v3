B. Additional policy for Washington good acquittance involving payment of Social Security
benefits not exceeding $1,000
1. Good acquittance
If the decedent was entitled to benefits under Title II of the Act, and, not less
than thirty days after the decedent’s death, SSA pays such benefits, not exceeding
$1,000, to a “qualifying successor,” the payment will be deemed to be made to the
decedent’s legal representative and releases SSA from liability to the same extent
as if payment had been made to an executor or administrator of the decedent’s estate.
Wash. Rev. Code § 11.66.010(1).
2. The following persons are “qualified successors”:
a.
the decedent’s surviving spouse;
b.
one or more of the deceased’s children, or their descendants;
c.
the secretary of social and health services (if the decedent was a resident of a state
institution at the date of death and liable for costs of care in an amount at least
as large as the amount of such benefits);
d.
a parent of the deceased; or
e.
a brother or sister of the deceased.
NOTE: If more than one request for payment is made by such persons, Washington requires
payment in the above order of preference.
3. Affidavit required
The “qualified successor” by whom or on whose behalf request for payment is made must
file an affidavit with the United States Department of Health, Education, and Welfare.
Wash. Rev. Code § 11.66.010(2).
The affidavit must show:
a.
the deceased’s date of death;
b.
the relationship of the affiant to the deceased;
c.
that no executor or administrator for the deceased has qualified or been appointed
and to the affiant’s knowledge no administration of the deceased’s estate is contemplated;
and
d.
to the affiant’s knowledge no relative of a closer degree of kindred to the deceased
than the affiant exists at the time of the filing of the affidavit.
Wash. Rev. Code § 11.66.010(2).
If the secretary of social and health services filed the affidavit, it must show:
a.
the deceased’s date of death;
b.
that no executor or administrator for the deceased has qualified or been appointed
and to the affiant’s knowledge no administration of the deceased’s estate is contemplated;
c.
the decedent left no known surviving spouse [or registered domestic partner] or children;
and
d.
the decedent died while a resident of a state institution at the date of death and
was liable for the cost of his or her care in an amount at least as large as the amount
of the benefits.
Wash. Rev. Code §§ 11.66.010(2), 11.66.900.
