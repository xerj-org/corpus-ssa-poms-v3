B. Administrative sanction case with earnings and overpayments
1.
QUESTION: The FO has a report and proof that the number holder (NH) worked. The NH has not
reported his work. If the FO asks the NH about working and he denies it, should the
FO have the NH sign a statement that they are not working prior to telling the NH
about the evidence we have?
ANSWER: The FO should not have the NH sign a statement prior to disclosing that we have evidence
of their work. At the time of disclosing the work evidence, inform the claimant that
the agency can impose sanctions if they knowingly make a false statement about working.
Also, advise the NH that knowingly failing to disclose future work might result in
a sanction. The FO should document and review the NH’s reason for not reporting the
work and determine if late reporting penalties or sanctions are appropriate.
IMPORTANT: Effective February 2021, Section 824 of the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015, provides that individuals who authorize Social Security to obtain records from a
payroll data provider shall not be subject to an administrative sanction for any omission
or error with respect to their wages as reported by the payroll data provider.
2.
QUESTION:
A Title II wage earner (WE) knowingly makes a false statement and the FO imposes a
sanction period. The WE has auxiliaries in pay (Ledger Account File (LAF) C) not affected
by the sanction. They returns to work during the sanction period and incur excess
earnings. Do we charge the excess earnings against the auxiliaries during the WE’s
sanction period?
ANSWER: Yes, we charge the excess earnings against the auxiliaries during the WE’s sanction
period. The amounts withheld from auxiliaries should be the same as they would be
if we were not sanctioning the WE.
