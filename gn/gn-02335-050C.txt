C. Child
The principles governing parent-child relationships are found in GN 00306.000ff. The
term “child” generally means:
1.
A person who would have the right under applicable State law to inherit intestate
personal property from the beneficiary as the natural legitimate child, or
2.
A person who was legally adopted by the beneficiary, or
3.
A person who is the beneficiary's stepchild.
In addition to the above, the following individual's may also qualify as “children” of a beneficiary:
4.
A child of an invalid ceremonial marriage which would have been a valid marriage except
for a legal impediment;
5.
An illegitimate child who has inheritance rights in the deceased beneficiary's estate,
or an illegitimate child who does not have such rights but, prior to the beneficiary's
death:
a.
Was acknowledged in writing by the beneficiary to be the child, or
b.
Was decreed by a court to be the beneficiary's child, or
c.
The beneficiary was ordered by a court to contribute to such child's support, or
d.
The deceased beneficiary is shown to be the parent of such child by other evidence
and was living with or contributing to the child's support at the time of death;
6.
A child who retains inheritance rights in the beneficiary's estate even though adopted
by another person during the beneficiary's lifetime;
7.
An equitably adopted child.
