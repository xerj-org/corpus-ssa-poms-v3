C. Policy — Acceptable Evidence Rebutting Presumption
1. General
You may use statements from neighbors, friends and relatives of the parties, or other
persons who know the facts.
CAUTION: See GN 00306.025 for applicability of Lord Mansfield Rule.
2. What Evidence Should Show
a.
Whether the mother and alleged father had a continuous relationship, lived together
continuously, and were considered husband and wife in their community.
b.
The whereabouts of the mother, her legal husband, and the alleged father during the
entire period when the child could have been conceived.
c.
The circumstances under which the alleged father and mother were together during the
period of conception (in cases where the legal husband was not constantly absent from
the mother during the whole period).
d.
When pertinent, whether the child was born prematurely or whether the gestation period
was normal or abnormal (based on the statement of the physician in attendance).
e.
Who was considered the natural father by the family group and those in a position
to know the facts when the child was born.
f.
Any other information that the situation warrants.
NOTE: The statement of a reputable physician must support any allegation that the mother's
husband was sterile at the applicable time. The physician's statement must show the
basis of his/her knowledge.
3. When to Submit to RCC
In the absence of a precedent opinion, submit the case to OGC per GN 01010.815 ff. if there is:
a.
A question as to whether certain types of evidence can be used to rebut the presumption;
or
b.
Evidence that the child was conceived by artificial insemination; or
c.
Evidence (not mere suspicion) indicating that the child is illegitimate (and would
not be entitled if illegitimate) and there is a question as to whether the evidence
is enough to rebut the presumption; or
d.
Doubt about what additional development is necessary.
