A. Domestic Claimants
1.
A domestic claimant is a person who lives in one of the following places:
a.
The 50 states
b.
Washington, D.C.
c.
Puerto Rico
d.
Virgin Islands
e.
Guam
f.
American Samoa
g.
The Northern Mariana Islands (for the SSI, HI/SMI, Special Age-72 Programs only.)
2.
The resident office is the servicing office of the principal claimant (or eligible
individual) or the person filing on behalf of the principle claimant (or eligible
individual). Any other DO is a nonresident office.
3.
In monthly benefit cases, the principle claimant is, in the order named:
a.
Number holder, eligible individual
b.
Widow, widower, or eligible spouse
c.
Youngest child for whom a claim will be filed
d.
Parent (father if both parents are filing)
4.
LSDP — No monthly benefits being paid—the principal claimant is:
a.
Widow or widower
b.
Child of NH
5.
If the person who would be principle claimant has not filed an application or is found
to be ineligible, the next in line will be the principal claimant. If the ineligible
person files an application, he/she will remain the principal claimant until his/her
claim is transmitted to the PSC, ODO, etc., in title II claims or is finally adjudicated
in Title XVI claims.
6.
If the principal claimant and a person filing on the claimant's behalf (representative
payee) are in different service areas, the DO servicing the representative payee's
address is the resident office until the principal claimant files a claim.
7.
Where the principal claimant's address is unknown, or where he/she is at a military
address not located within the United States, the address of the next claimant in
line determines the resident office. If there are no other claimants, the DO first
receiving his/her application will assume jurisdiction of the case.
