B. Procedure for court decrees
1. Extract translations of court decrees
SSA translators who are claims representatives and translators in the Translation
and Priority Workload Unit (TPWU) extract translations from court decrees. Include
all of the pertinent information from the decrees including the following:
a.
The name and address of the court issuing the decree.
b.
The type of decree (i.e., final divorce, interlocutory decree, annulment, adoption,
or other decree).
c.
The issuing date of the decree.
d.
Names and addresses of the parties to the legal action.
e.
If one or both parties did not appear in person before the court, show:
•
The name of each absent party;
•
Who represented the absent party;
•
How service was performed for the notice of the impending action on the absent party;
and
•
Whether the absent party filed a signed statement consenting to the action.
f.
The exact wording of the portion of the decree indicating the divorce action, the
marriage annulment, the child adoption, or other issue.
g.
Any restrictions placed on either or both of the parties.
h.
Whether the decree was final upon issuance or depended upon other action (publication,
filing a record, or other action.)
2. Verbatim translations of court decrees
a.
Exercise discretion and translate any other portions of the decree that might be pertinent
to the claim.
b.
Prepare a verbatim translation if you believe the extract translation would be insufficient
to make a determination on the claim. Similarly, the claims technical examiner (CTE)
may request a verbatim translation when they believe the extract translation is insufficient
to adjudicate the claim.
