SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 00504.105 - Direct Payment to Incapable Beneficiaries When Further Payee Development is Needed
Effective Dates: 07/19/2017 - Present | TN 35 (03-25)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0200504105 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Examples of Substantial Harm Determinations
The following are examples of substantial harm determinations:
1. Substantial Harm Not Established
A claim is approved for a title II or XVI claimant who suffers from a mental impairment.
During the interview, it becomes obvious that the beneficiary will need a payee; however,
one has not been found when the claim is ready to be adjudicated. While the CR believes
that the beneficiary may not use the money wisely, there is no indication that direct
receipt of the funds would cause physical or mental injury. In fact, suspending benefits
could result in the beneficiary having no food.
Since the CR has determined that substantial harm would not result from direct payment,
they should make direct payment and continue payee development. Input the payee issue
on the MBR/SSR per prior instructions (e.g., “UTL PYE-DIR PAY PENDING PYE DEV”). Also, input “Interim Direct Payment” on the Report of Contact Details screen of
eRPS.
2. Substantial Harm Established
You are unable to find a payee for a 67 year old retirement beneficiary who is an
alcoholic. Based on contacts with the doctor and beneficiary, you determine that the
beneficiary has been hospitalized recently for their drinking. Paying the beneficiary
directly will cause injury, so you may establish that substantial harm could result.
Suspend payment based on the substantial harm rule (documenting your decision on an
RC) while you develop for a payee. Remember, if a payee is not found in 1 month, direct
payment must be initiated. Input the reason for suspense to the MBR per prior instructions
(e.g., PYE REFUSES TO SERVE-SUB HARM EST). Also, once direct payment is initiated,
input “Interim Direct
Payment” on the RPRC screen of eRPS (MS 07416.002).
