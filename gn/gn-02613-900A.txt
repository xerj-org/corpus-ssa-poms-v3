SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 02613.900 - Offense Code Categories for Crimes
Effective Dates: 08/02/2022 - Present | TN 40 (01-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0202613900 (fetched 2026-10-04)

A. Background
Beginning in April 2009, under the Martinez nationwide class action settlement, the
agency agreed that it would only suspend Title II benefits or suspend or deny Title
XVI benefits based on a felony arrest warrant if the warrant was for one of the three
felony offense codes that refer expressly to flight or escape (i.e., offense codes
4901, 4902, and 4999). The settlement did not affect the policy for such nonpayments
in the case of probation or parole violators.
1. Violent crimes
The Department of Justice provides information on crimes through a number of sources
including the Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS) and the FBI. BJS defines violent
offenses as those that are “threatening, attempting, or actually using physical force
against a person.” Crimes listed as “violent” by BJS are murder, negligent manslaughter,
assault, robbery, sexual abuse, kidnapping, threatening communication, and threats
against the President.
The FBI, while not categorizing crimes as “violent,” reports crime statistics in a
manner consistent with the BJS definition. In both the Uniform Crime Report and the
National Incident-Based Reporting System (NIBRS), the FBI categorizes crimes that
threaten, attempt, or actually use physical force against a person as assault, homicide,
kidnapping/abduction, robbery, and forcible sex offenses.
NIBRS derived its two-digit offense codes from the FBI’s four-digit National Crime
Information Center (NCIC) Uniform Offense Classification Codes. “Violent” crimes are
reported in the 0900, 1000, 1100, 1200, and 1300 series of the NCIC offense codes.
Offense codes representing communication threatening use of physical force are 1602,
2101, 5215, and 5216.
NOTE: For probation or parole violators, the NCIC code will frequently be 5011, 5012, 8101
or 8102. These codes generically identify the case as a probation or parole violation
and do not provide specific information concerning the nature of the original offense
or the nature (i.e., non violent or not drug related, etc.) of the probation or parole
violation. The facts regarding the nature of the probation or parole violation cause
may be available on FFAP or on the FFSCF. If there is no additional information provided
in FFAP or on the FFSCF the information will have to be obtained from the beneficiary,
if necessary, because they are requesting good cause.
2. Drug-related abuse crimes
The FBI, in its NIBRS guidelines, defines drug-related crimes as “The unlawful cultivation,
manufacture, distribution, sale, purchase, use, possession, transportation, or importation
of any controlled drug or narcotic substance.” Drug crimes are reported in the 3500
series of the NCIC offense codes.
