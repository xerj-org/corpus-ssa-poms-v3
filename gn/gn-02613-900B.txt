B. Exhibit – list of violent, drug related and nonviolent and not drug related offense
codes
1. List of NCIC crime codes
The following list represents a compilation of criminal offense codes that the Department
of Justice has determined to be violent or drug-related or nonviolent and not drug
related. The violent crimes are coded as a “V” on the chart and the drug-related crimes
are coded as “D”. Additionally, the offense codes listed on the chart that do not
have a value of “D” or “V” represent non-violent or not drug related criminal offenses.
2. Using the NCIC crime code chart
Steps
Actions
1
If the 4-digit offense code on the FFWQ screen is one of the codes listed on the chart,
the criminal offense on the beneficiary’s warrant can be determined to be:
(V) - violent
(D) - drug related
(blank) - nonviolent or not drug related
2
If the specific 4-digit offense code from the FFWQ screen is not listed on one of
the charts in GN 02613.900B.2.b. or GN 02613.900B.3., check to see if the first two digits of the 4-digit offense code can be found in
the chart at GN 02613.900B.2.a.
3
If the first 2 digits of the 4-digit offense code are the same two digits reflected
in the chart at GN 02613.900B.2.a., the criminal offense is considered to be violent or drug related.
4
If the 4-digit offense code from the FFWQ is not listed in the chart at GN 02613.900B.2.a. or GN 02613.900B.3. or in the chart in GN 02613.900B.2.b., then the criminal offense must be developed per GN 02613.175B.1.
NOTE: If the offense code information on the FFWQ is “blank” or “9999” it means that no
offense code was provided by the reporter. Code 9999 or blank should not be interpreted as a crime that is not violent or not drug related. Refer the case
to OIG via email (^Fugitive Felon) to request assistance in determining the appropriate
NCIC offense code. In the Subject Line of the email, include your District Office
(DO) or Processing Center (PC) code. For additional instructions on referring warrants
to OIG, see GN 02613.150B.1.

NOTE: Some of the warrant referrals received from law enforcement list "driving under the
influence" as a charge on the warrant. If the warrant lists a charge of "driving under
the influence," you must determine if the charge is related to the individual's alcohol
consumption or drug consumption. (See GN 02613.175B.1.a. for instructions on developing for missing or incomplete NCIC offense codes.) If
the charge is based on alcohol consumption, the charge is not a drug-related crime.
If the individual has a "driving while under the influence" charge that is drug related,
refer to GN 02613.900B.2.b. for the applicable NCIC offense codes for drug related crimes.
