SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 00306.021 - Rebuttal of Presumption of Legitimacy
Effective Dates: 11/16/2023 - Present | TN 75 (11-23)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0200306021 (fetched 2026-10-04)

A. Policy — General Rule
In general, the presumption of legitimacy is rebutted if clear and convincing evidence
shows that:
•
The husband was sterile (unless the child was conceived by artificial insemination);
or
•
The husband was constantly absent from his wife during the whole period within which
the child must have been conceived; or
•
The husband was present with his wife only under circumstances which made sexual relations
impossible; or
•
The child's mother was living in adultery continuously during the time the child must
have been conceived and there is no evidence of access by the husband.
NOTES:
•
Additional grounds for rebutting the presumption exist in some States (e.g., blood/genetic
tests showing that the wife's husband could not have been the child's father). In
the absence of a legal precedent opinion on this issue, submit the case to the Office
of the General Counsel (OGC) for an opinion per GN 01010.815 ff.
•
Also, see GN 00306.021B for presumption of legitimacy in Louisiana, California, and Oregon.
