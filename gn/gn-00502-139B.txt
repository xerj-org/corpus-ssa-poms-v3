SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 00502.139 - Additional Development/Considerations When Guardian Involved
Effective Dates: 04/15/2016 - Present | TN 84 (05-23)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0200502139 (fetched 2026-10-04)

B. Procedure
1. Legal Guardian/Conservator
If you learn that the beneficiary has a court-appointed legal guardian:
•
obtain proof of appointment, and
•
advise the guardian of the beneficiary’s entitlement to benefits and of a guardian’s
right to file to be appointed payee.
REMINDER: You are not required to appoint the legal guardian as payee. Appoint the person who will best serve the beneficiary.
2. Legal Guardian/Conservator Appointed After Payee Named
If a court appoints a legal guardian after a payee is already appointed for the beneficiary,
notify the guardian of the name and address of the payee. If the guardian files to
be payee, appoint the guardian only if they can better serve the beneficiary’s interests.
REMEMBER: Correct the payee coding to reflect the change in guardianship. For Correct RP Coding
–MBR/SSR/eRPS processing instructions see MS INTRANETERPS 019.005.
3. Statutory Guardian
Obtain proof of appointment for a statutory guardian.
NOTE: The conditions under which the appointment was made can help you determine the beneficiary’s
capability and the statutory guardian’s suitability as a payee.
4. Voluntary Conservator
Appointing a payee is never made simply as a convenience or at the choice of the beneficiary,
however if the beneficiary has not been determined to be incapable and wants a voluntary
conservator appointed as payee:
•
obtain a signed statement from the beneficiary requesting the appointment of the conservator
as representative payee,
•
obtain proof of the court-appointment of the conservator,
•
obtain a payee application from the conservator (For the representative payee application
see GN 00502.107B.2.), and
•
appoint the conservator as payee without further payee development.
CAUTION: If at any time you obtain/receive evidence that shows the appointment is not in the
beneficiary’s best interests, pay the beneficiary directly or initiate capability/payee
development.
5. Power of Attorney
If you learn that the beneficiary has executed a power of attorney appointing a third
party to act as their agent to perform certain specified tasks, explain to the third
party agent that a power of attorney does not authorize the agent to negotiate the
beneficiary’s benefit checks and that the agent needs to file a payee application
(For representative payee application see GN 00502.107). Obtain proof of the power of attorney appointment and determine the beneficiary’s
capability.
Appoint the holder of a beneficiary’s power of attorney if they is in the best position
to serve the beneficiary. (For the payee preference list, see GN 00502.105).
