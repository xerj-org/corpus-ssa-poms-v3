SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 00302.327 - Definition of Holocaust Survivor
Effective Dates: 12/21/1999 - Present | 
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0200302327 (fetched 2026-10-04)

E. Example 3
The claimant alleged being born in Poland on 6/5/09. All documentary evidence of age
showed a DB of 6/5/19. No public or religious record of birth exists.
The claimant stated that he was a Jew who was imprisoned in the Soviet Union in 1940
for his political and religious activities. After being taken prisoner by Russian
troops who overrun eastern Poland, he changed his DB to make himself younger so that
he would be sent to a work camp rather than to a Soviet prison camp. As evidence of
survivor status, he submitted a record, dated 11/45, from an Austrian displaced-person's
camp.
In this case, SSA cannot use the special Holocaust procedures to establish the claimant's
DB because the claimant does not qualify as a survivor for purposes of this provision.
The claimant was a member of a group targeted by the Nazis for ex termination and
had proof of residence in a Nazi-dominat ed country prior to 1946 (normally acceptable
as evidence of survivor status) and was, in fact, incarcerated during World War II.
Nevertheless, the facts show that he was not imprisoned by the Nazis and did not misstate
his DB to avoid Nazi persecution, confinement in a Nazi concentration camp, or other
means of death at the hands of the Nazis.
Proof of age must, therefore, be established under the usual rules.
