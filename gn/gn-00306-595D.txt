SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - GN 00306.595 - Ohio Intestacy Laws
Effective Dates: 10/13/2005 - Present | TN 29 (10-05)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0200306595 (fetched 2026-10-04)

D. Under the Ohio Parentage Act, the SSA adjudicator may consider other facts to determine
whether a parent-child relationship has been established. After a putative father's
death, paternity may be established by clear and convincing evidence. Clear and convincing
evidence requires more certainty on the part of the fact-finder than a “preponderance
of the evidence” standard (more than 50%), but less certainty than the “beyond a reasonable
doubt” standard. During a putative father's life, paternity is established by a preponderance
of the evidence. Evidence relating to paternity may include:
1. evidence of sexual intercourse between the mother and the alleged father at any
possible time of conception; or
2. an expert's opinion concerning the statistical probability of the alleged father's
paternity, which opinion is based upon the duration of the mother's pregnancy; or
3. genetic test results, weighed in accordance with evidence, if available, of the
statistical probability of the alleged father's paternity; or
4. medical evidence relating to the alleged father's paternity of the child based
on tests performed by experts; or
5. all other evidence that is relevant to the issue of paternity of the child.
