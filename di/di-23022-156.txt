DI 23022.156 Esthesioneuroblastoma
COMPASSIONATE ALLOWANCES INFORMATION
ESTHESIONEUROBLASTOMA
ALTERNATE NAMES
Intracranial Olfactory Neuroblastoma; Olfactory Neuroblastoma; Recurrent Esthesioneuroblastoma;
Skull Based Olfactory Neuroblastoma
DESCRIPTION
Esthesioneuroblastoma is a rare cancer of the upper part of the nasal cavity called the cribriform plate,
which is a bone deep in the skull between the eyes, and above the ethmoid sinuses.
It develops in nerve tissue associated with the sense of smell and can occur in people
of any age. This cancer is very uncommon, accounting for 7% of all cancers of the
nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses. Although it generally grows slowly, an esthesioneuroblastoma
can sometimes grow very quickly. Fast-growing tumors can metastasize (spread) even
many years after treatment of the initial tumor.
DIAGNOSTIC TESTING, PHYSICAL FINDINGS, AND
ICD-9-CM/ICD-10-CM
CODING
Diagnostic testing:
•
Physical examination of the eyes, nose and throat;
•
Endoscopy examination of the mouth and nose;
•
Computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), or positron emission tomography
(PET) scan;
•
Biopsy; or
•
Laboratory testing of the blood and urine.
Physical findings: People with an esthesioneuroblastoma may present with:
•
Nasal obstruction;
•
Nosebleeds (epistaxis);
•
Changes in the sense of smell (hyposmia);
•
Nasal discharge (pus);
•
Facial pain;
•
Changes in vision;
•
Excessive tearing from the eyes (lacrimation);
•
Facial or tooth numbness;
•
Ear pain or pressure;
•
Enlarged lymph nodes in the neck; and
•
Difficulty opening the mouth.

ICD-10: C30.0
PROGRESSION
Esthesioneuroblastoma usually begins in the part of the brain that interprets smell
(olfactory bulb). This tumor may occur at any age, and tends to form behind the nose
and may affect the sinuses. People with olfactory neuroblastomas may lose the sense
of smell, have frequent nosebleeds, and may experience difficulties breathing through
their nose.
TREATMENT
Esthesioneuroblastomas are treated with surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy depending
on the response to treatment and metastasis. These treatments may affect eating, speaking,
or breathing.
