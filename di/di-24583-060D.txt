D. How do we use age- and grade-equivalent scores when evaluating children?
Some test reports, particular reports from achievement tests, contain information
about age and grade equivalence.
1. Definitions
a. Raw score
A raw score is the unconverted score on a standardized test.
b. Age-equivalent score
An age-equivalent score is the average age of children who received the same raw score
on a standardized test.
c. Grade-equivalent score
A grade-equivalent score is the grade and month of the school year that is the average
grade placement for students who received the same raw score on a standardized test.
2. Use of age- and grade-equivalent scores
Age- and grade-equivalent scores represent average scores and are not standard scores
that can be converted to standard deviations. Because we compare the child to other
children their age, an age- or grade-equivalent score by itself has limited usefulness
since it does not indicate how well the child did in comparison to other children
their age who do not have impairments. Do not rely on any test score alone. Consider
the information from the test along with all the other evidence. These scores, along
with other information, may give a picture of the child's overall progress in development
or learning.
