B. Permanent and Total Disability
A person is “permanently and totally disabled” if he has a disability which meets the conditions specified in 3141.31 and 3141.32.
1.
“Permanent disability” (3141.31) means any physical or mental impairment that is substantiated by a medical
diagnosis and which has lasted or is expected to last for a continuous period of not
less than 12 months, or which can be expected to result in death.
Alcoholism and drug addiction, like other anatomical, physiological or psychological
abnormalities which meet the above criteria, are permanent disabilities.
2.
“Total disability” (3141.32) means inability to engage in any substantial gainful activity that exists in the community.
“Substantial gainful activity” is any work of a nature generally performed for remuneration or profit, involving
the performance of significant physical or mental duties or a combination of both.
