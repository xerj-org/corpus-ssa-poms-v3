E. How we use genetic test results
Consider all medical evidence, including the results of genetic tests, when evaluating
a disability claim. We consider genetic tests objective medical evidence.
1. Requirements for obtaining genetic test results
Request genetic test results when developing a claim for disability consistent with
our policies in DI 22505.001 Medical Evidence of Record (MER) Policies. We do not require genetic test results
to find a claimant disabled. We will not purchase genetic testing as part of a consultative
examination.
2. Establishing an MDI
Genetic test results can help establish an MDI if it is from an AMS and not based
on DTC testing. However, clinical signs and symptoms must be consistent with the MDI.
IMPORTANT: DTC test results cannot establish an MDI regardless of the endorsement or involvement
of an AMS. See DI
24515.079D.
3. Meeting a Listing
Several listings include a discussion of genetic test results, for example listings
8.07A, 108.07A, 10.06A, 10.06B, 110.06A, and 110.06B. With the exception of non-mosaic Down syndrome, genetic test results alone are not
sufficient to make a disability determination or decision. For details, see:
•
DI 34001.028 Congenital Disorders That Affect Multiple Body Systems
•
DI 34005.110 Congenital Disorders that Affect Multiple Body Systems
4. Providing information about impairment severity
Genetic test results generally do not provide significant information about impairment
severity or functional capacity. However, it can help evaluate the consistency and
supportability of alleged symptoms and limitations.
