SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - DI 33015.032 - Continuance of Disability Hearing
Effective Dates: 06/08/2023 - Present | TN 24 (09-25)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0433015032 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Procedure for continuance request
1. Beneficiary (or recipient) or representative fails to appear
a.
Ask the individual who has appeared to explain why the beneficiary (or recipient)
or representative failed to appear.
b.
If the beneficiary (or recipient) failed to appear, refer to DI 33015.021.
NOTE: An appointed representative may not testify in place of the beneficiary (or recipient)
at a hearing. See GN 03910.025A.
c.
If the representative failed to appear, explore the possibility of holding the hearing
with the beneficiary (or recipient) who has appeared. Clarify that you will continue
the hearing at a later date if the beneficiary (or recipient) does not wish to proceed
or if you believe the representative is crucial to that hearing.
Example: A beneficiary's (or recipient's) attorney does not appear—beneficiary (or recipient)
agrees to begin the hearing without the attorney; however, during the hearing, the
beneficiary (or recipient) states they do not wish to continue without the attorney.
Adjourn and reconvene the hearing so the attorney can be present.
d.
Use judgment in deciding whether good cause exists to grant another continuance if
more than one has already been granted.
2. Witness fails to appear
a.
Explain that the hearing is the necessary first step in determining if witness testimony
is needed if the beneficiary (or recipient) or appointed representative refuses to
proceed with the hearing. Also, explain that if the hearing is not held, the case
will be decided on the record. Ensure that everyone understands the consequences of
refusing to proceed;
b.
Hold the hearing as scheduled;
c.
Decide at the conclusion of the hearing whether a continuance is warranted to obtain
testimony from the absent witness(es); and
d.
Consider the following factors in deciding whether to grant a continuance for the
witness(es). Use these factors as a balancing test and use discretion and judgment
in making the decision:
•
Whether there is good cause for why the witness(es) did not attend the hearing (e.g.,
emergency, surgery, etc.). Determine when the beneficiary (or recipient) or appointed
representative was aware that the witness(es) could not attend and whether an attempt
was made to postpone the hearing; and
•
Whether the testimony of the non-appearing witness(es) is material to the disability
determination based on a review of the entire record, including the evidence provided
at the hearing. This factor may be especially important in the case of expert witnesses
(e.g., the DHO may find that a continuance is warranted to obtain a physician’s testimony
on particular points in the adjudication process that may not be reflected in previously
submitted medical reports).
3. If continuance not granted
a.
Annotate the SSA-1204-BK or SSA-1205-BK to reflect that a continuance was requested,
who requested it, and the reason the continuance was not granted; and
b.
Proceed with the determination as appropriate.
4. If continuance granted
a.
Document the claims folder to reflect that the case was continued, who requested the
continuance, and the reason the continuance was granted;
b.
Schedule another hearing for the earliest date possible; and
c.
Assign the case to the same DHO if a substantive action was taken.
