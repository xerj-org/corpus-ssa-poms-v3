A. Terms used in the reconsideration process
1. Reconsideration
The reconsideration is the first step in the administrative review process that the
Social Security Administration (SSA) provides to a claimant dissatisfied with an initial
medical determination or adverse medical reopening (AMR). An individual, e.g., an
auxiliary, who shows in writing that their rights are adversely affected by an initial
medical determination or AMR may also request a reconsideration.
The reconsideration involves a thorough, independent review of all evidence from the
initial determination and any new evidence the claimant or their appointed representative,
parent of minor child, or legal guardian submits, or we obtain, in connection with
the request for reconsideration.
For more information on:
•
The reconsideration process, see GN 03102.100
•
The types of AMRs afforded a disability hearing, see DI
29001.001B.
2. Request for reconsideration
A request for reconsideration can be expressed or implied; however, it must be in
writing. A written request for reconsideration is any documentation (e.g., a letter,
facsimile, or submission of additional evidence) that clearly implies disagreement
with the initial medical determination.
For more information on:
•
Who may request a reconsideration, see GN
03102.100C.2.
•
What constitutes a written request for a reconsideration, see GN
03102.100C.3.
•
What is not a request for reconsideration, see GN
03102.100C.4.
•
The time period to request a reconsideration, see GN
03102.100C.6.
•
Implied requests for reconsideration, see DI
81020.025B.
3. Adjudicative team
An adjudicative team consisting of a disability examiner (DE) and a medical consultant
(MC) or psychological consultant (PC) make the reconsideration determination. The
DE and MC/PC involved in the reconsideration determination must be different from
the persons who made the initial determination. For more information on the adjudicative
team, see DI
24501.001B.
NOTE: A Medical Advisor (MA) is not part of the adjudicative team but can provide analysis
of medical issues at both the initial and reconsideration level of adjudication. See
DI
24501.001B.6
