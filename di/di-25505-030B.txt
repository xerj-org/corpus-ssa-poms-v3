B. When and how to determine expected duration
When adjudicating a claim within 12 months of the date the claimant's MDI(s) first
prevented SGA, determine the “expected duration” of the claimants MDI(s), if necessary, by projecting whether the MDI(s) is expected
to result in death or continue to be severe and
prevent performance of SGA for a continuous period of not less than 12 months. In
Title XVI child cases, project whether the MDI(s) will continue to be so severe that
it causes marked and severe functional limitations.
Consider the nature and course of the MDI(s), as well as the claimants treatment history,
by evaluating the following:
•
symptoms, signs, and laboratory findings;
•
frequency, dosage, and effects of medication;
•
surgery, radiation, or chemotherapy; and
•
treatment response, such as improvement or worsening in symptoms, signs, and laboratory
findings.
Longitudinal evidence is extremely important and necessary when evaluating the severity
of a mental MDI(s). A claimants level of functioning can vary considerably over time.
It is vital to obtain evidence from relevant sources over a sufficiently long period
prior to the date of adjudication to establish severity and probable duration of the
MDI(s). For more information on developing longitudinal evidence, see DI 22505.010.
