SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - DI 27501.001 - Medical Reopenings and Revisions: Pertinent Definitions and Related Policy
Effective Dates: 09/24/2026 - Present | TN 4 (09-26)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0427501001 (fetched 2026-10-04)

A. Definitions
1. Medical reopening
A medical reopening is a formal review of an administratively final determination
or decision to admit new and material medical evidence or to reevaluate the evidence
used in the prior determination or decision. A reopening generally results in a more
favorable determination or decision although an adverse medical reopening is occasionally
appropriate.
For additional information on when reopening is appropriate see DI 27501.005.
NOTE: The term “determination” refers to adjudications at the initial and reconsideration
levels, and “decision” refers to adjudications at the Administrative Law Judge (ALJ)
and Appeals Council (AC) levels.
a. Adverse Medical Reopening (AMR)
An AMR is reopening of an administratively final initial or reconsideration-level
allowance determination or continuing disability review (CDR) continuance based on
medical factors that revise the determination to a denial, cessation, later onset,
or closed period determination.
An AMR does not includes (1) adverse reopenings based on non-medical factors, such
as substantial gainful activity (SGA) or field office (FO) determinations, or (2)
medical reopenings that are not adverse, such as a reopening of a denial resulting
in an allowance or a more favorable outcome.
An AMR does include cases that are reopened due to fraud or similar fault that involve
medical matters. This does not include non-medical issues outside of DDS jurisdiction
such as SGA and unreported work activity.
b. Reconsideration of an Adverse Medical Reopening (RAMR)
A RAMR occurs when an individual requests a reconsideration of an AMR. An individual
has the right to request a reconsideration and receive a disability hearing if they
have been receiving disability benefits and we make an initial or revised determination
based on medical factors that the individual is not disabled because the impairment
ceased, did not exist, or is no longer disabling.
A Disability Hearing Officer (DHO) review is not afforded to an individual who requests a RAMR for a less favorable onset. If an individual
requests a RAMR for a less favorable onset, we follow the regular reconsideration
process covered in DI 12005.000 and DI 27001.000. An appeal of an unfavorable RAMR for onset may ultimately result in an Administrative
Law Judge (ALJ) review.
2. Revision
Any change in the end result or basis of a final determination or decision.
3. Final determination or decision
A determination or decision (initial or revised) is final as of the date of the notice
unless:
a.
it is timely appealed (or a civil action is filed); or
b.
it is appealed late but good cause for late filing of an appeal or civil action is
found; or
c.
the Appeals Council (AC) takes jurisdiction on its own motion within 60 days of the
date of the Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) decision; or
d.
the AC denies a request for review (does not issue a decision), in which case the
ALJ decision is the final decision; or
e.
it is reopened and revised.
NOTE: Form SSA-L1157
Social Security Notice and SSA-L1157-SI Supplement Security Notice
- are interim notices and not final notices.
