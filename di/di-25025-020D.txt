SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - DI 25025.020 - Applying the Medical-Vocational Rules When the Claimant Has Exertional and Nonexertional Limitations
Effective Dates: 02/13/2015 - Present | TN 14 (03-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0425025020 (fetched 2026-10-04)

D. Significantly eroded sedentary occupational base or inability to sustain a 40-hour
workweek
1. When the applicable sedentary medical-vocational rule directs a finding of disability
Use that rule to direct a finding of disabled. See “Using the Medical Vocational Guidelines”
in DI 25025.005.
2. When the applicable sedentary medical-vocational rule does not direct a finding of
disabled
Use the sedentary rule that comes closest to the claimant’s medical-vocational factors
of age, education, and past work experience as a framework for a determination, even
though the decision for that rule is “not disabled.” Explain why you are finding the
claimant’s limitations significantly erode the occupational base for sedentary work,
or why you are finding the claimant is not capable of sustaining a 40-hour workweek.
For additional guidance on how to make these types of determinations, see:
•
DI 25015.020 Determining Capability to Do Other Work - Implications of a Residual Functional Capacity
for Less Than a Full Range of Sedentary Work; and
•
DI 24510.057 Sustainability and the Residual Functional Capacity Assessment.
