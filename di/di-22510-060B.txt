SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - DI 22510.060 - Pediatric Consultative Examination (CE) Report Content Guidelines for Speech and Language Impairments in Children Age 3 and Older
Effective Dates: 01/28/2026 - Present | TN 62 (01-26)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0422510060 (fetched 2026-10-04)

B. Report content specific to pediatric speech and language impairments
The CE provider will describe and discuss, as appropriate:
1.
New information per caregiver or child report which is not in the medical history
or any information reported that is inconsistent with records already in the file;
2.
Previous, current, or planned interventions (e.g., speech/language therapy, cleft
lip/palate repair, pressure equalizing tube insertion, special education classroom
program);
3.
Language(s) spoken in the home and the child's preferred language;
4.
Whether the CE was conducted in person or via telehealth (for children over age 5).
If a telehealth CE, document confirmation that the child could clearly see any visual
stimuli presented;
5.
Whether the speech-language pathologist (SLP) is familiar with the child;
6.
Information obtained by parent or child report rather than by direct clinical observation
and assessment, which the SLP must clearly indicate as such;
7.
Any test(s) and subtests administered. Document deviations from the administration
procedures outlined in the test manual; and
8.
When formal testing cannot be completed or is inappropriate for the child. In these
situations, the SLP must conduct informal speech or language testing, clearly document
the reason for informal testing, and ask the child to complete language probes/tasks
that are age appropriate (e.g., asking the child to name/describe photographs, sequence
pictures, or real objects; asking the child to follow directions for manipulating
blocks/objects; obtaining a speech/language sample based on the child's interests
and during the devised informal assessment).
NOTE:
The general expectation is that the transition to using the latest version of a test
occurs no more than one year after publication.
