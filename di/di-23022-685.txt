DI 23022.685 Carcinoma of Unknown Primary Site
COMPASSIONATE ALLOWANCES INFORMATION
CARCINOMA OF UNKNOWN PRIMARY SITE
ALTERNATE NAMES
Cancer of Unknown Primary Origin; CUP; Malignant Neoplasms of Unknown Origin; Malignant
Neoplasms of Unknown Site; Occult Primary Cancer; Occult Primary Malignancy; Origin
of Unknown Primary Site
DESCRIPTION
Carcinoma of
unknown
primary
site (CUP) is a condition where cancer cells are found in the body, but the place where the cells
first started growing (the origin or primary site) cannot be determined. The clinical
presentation of CUP origin is extremely variable, and depends on the extent and type
of organ involvement.
Most individuals with CUP present with multiple areas of involvement in multiple visceral
sites (organs in the cavities of the body), the most common sites of involvement being
lung, bone, lymph nodes, and liver. Individuals with CUP are presumed to all have
stage IV disease at the time of initial presentation.
DIAGNOSTIC TESTING, PHYSICAL FINDINGS, AND
ICD-9-CM/ICD-10-CM
CODING
Diagnostic testing: Diagnostic testing for CUP includes biopsy, imaging studies, and laboratory analysis.
Physical findings: Individuals with CUP may have:
•
Long-lasting pain in a specific area of the body;
•
Loss of appetite;
•
Unexplained weight loss;
•
A cough or hoarseness that doesn’t go away;
•
Thickening or lump in any part of the body;
•
Changes in bowel or bladder habits;
•
Unusual bleeding or discharge; and
•
Recurring fever or night sweats.
ICD-9: 199.1
ICD-10: C80.1
PROGRESSION
The incidence of CUP increases with age, with the median age on presentation for both
men and women ranging from 59-66 years. The overall prognosis of individuals with
CUP with multiple organ involvement and poor response to treatment is grave.
TREATMENT
The treatment for CUP is dependent on organ involvement.
