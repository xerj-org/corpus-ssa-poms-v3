A. Policy — ISM In Non-institutional Care Living Arrangements
1. Non-institutional Care
A non-institutional care situation exists when all of the following conditions are
met:
a.
An individual is placed by a public or private agency under a specific program of
protective placement such as foster or family care;
b.
The placement is in a private household (not an institution or commercial establishment)
which is licensed or otherwise approved by the State to provide protective care;
c.
The placing agency retains responsibility for continuing supervision of the need for
placement and of the services provided; and
d.
The individual, the placing agency, or some other party pays for the services provided,
or has a written agreement to pay for the services provided.
2. ISM Rules for Non-institutional Care
a.
An individual in non-institutional care is always considered to be living in their
own household and the VTR cannot apply. ISM is valued under the PMV rule.
b.
An eligible couple placed in non-institutional care is considered to be living in
their own household separate from others living in the same residence.
c.
Any other person residing in the same dwelling is in a separate household from the
individual/couple in non-institutional care.
d.
If an individual is placed under the provisions of a State foster care plan with their
deemor, deeming is applicable.
NOTE: Any ISM provided by a person from whom income is deemed for the month has already
been taken into consideration through the deeming process, and thus, ISM would not
be charged from such a deemor.
3. Care Rate and ISM Determinations
a.
The care rate established by the placing agency is the CMV of whatever food, and shelter
that the care provider furnishes.
b.
If the individual pays the CMV of the food, and shelter with their own funds, no ISM
is countable.
c.
If the individual incurs indebtedness for the CMV of the food, and shelter, no ISM
is countable.
d.
Any payment by the individual is presumed to be for the food, and shelter provided.
NOTE: In general, make the presumption that any payment made by the individual to the non-institutional
care provider is a payment towards the CMV of the food and shelter. However, there
may be instances (e.g., impairment-related work expenses (IRWE), plans for achieving
self-support) when it might not be to the individual's advantage to apply this presumption.
In such a situation, the individual's payment should be attributed in the manner that
is most advantageous to the individual (SI 00835.790B., Example 2).
4. Care Provider Receives No Foster Care Payment
In some cases, State foster care agencies make non-institutional care placements in
which the care provider does not receive a foster care payment or other reimbursement.
Typically, this occurs when the child is placed with a relative who agrees to care
for the child without being reimbursed.
a.
If the placement meets the requirements in SI 00835.790A.1. (except for the payment requirement in SI 00835.790A.1.d.), the individual is considered to be in non-institutional care.
b.
Because this individual is considered to be in non-institutional care, they are considered
to be living in their own household.
c.
Because the individual is living in their own household, the one-third reduction rule
(VTR) does not apply. ISM is determined using the PMV rule.
d.
The food, and shelter supplied by the care provider is countable ISM unless the individual
pays the provider for it. See SI 00835.790B.4. for the procedures for determining ISM.
