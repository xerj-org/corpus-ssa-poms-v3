L. Examples of how to treat the first-time homebuyer tax credit and deemed first-time
homebuyer tax credit for SSI income purposes
1. SSI claimant receives a tax refund equal to or greater than the tax credit before
January 1, 2010
Rodney Smith purchased their first home for $64,000 in December 2008. Rodney alleges
qualifying for a $6,400 first-time homebuyer tax credit that they claimed on their
federal tax return. Rodney filed their return with IRS in April 2009. During Rodney's
SSI redetermination, they stated that they received a federal tax refund in June 2009
for $7,000. Rodney presented their settlement statement that verified the purchase
price of the home as $64,000. Based on these facts, Rodney is ineligible for SSI in
June 2009 because the $6,400 tax credit they received in that month counts as unearned
income. We consider the additional $600 refund Rodney received to be a refund of taxes
already paid; the additional $600 is not income for SSI purposes.
2. SSI claimant receives a tax refund that is less than the value of the tax credit before
January 1, 2010
Coral Jones, an SSI claimant with an ineligible spouse, alleges during their redetermination
interview that they received a federal tax refund of $2,000 in February 2009. Coral
filed a joint Federal tax return in January 2009 and claimed the first-time homebuyer
tax credit of $8,000. Coral presented a copy of their settlement statement that verified
the home’s purchase price as $90,000. Since The couple's tax liability was less than
the full tax credit due, they received a refund of $2,000. Coral is ineligible for
SSI in February, since we consider one-half of the refund ($1,000) as unearned income.
The other half of the refund ($1,000) is Coral's unearned income in February 2009.
3. SSI couple receives the tax credit as deemed first-time homebuyers after January 1,
2010
An SSI couple lived in their home for 25 years. They sold their home for $30,000 in
January 2010 and purchased a new home for $68,000 in the same month. They filed a
joint federal tax return in January 2010 to claim the $6,500 deemed first-time homebuyer
tax credit. The couple had zero tax liability. The $6,500 tax credit they received
in May 2010 is not countable income for SSI purposes. They retained the $6,500 until
they spent it to make repairs to their home in August 2010. The retained amount is
not a countable resource during this period, and they continued to be eligible for
SSI.
