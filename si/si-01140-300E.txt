SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 01140.300 - Promissory Notes and Property Agreements
Effective Dates: 05/22/2003 - Present | TN 70 (12-23)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0501140300 (fetched 2026-10-04)

E. Examples - cases
1. Contract for sale
Situation: Dottle, an SSI applicant, sold a parcel of land to a neighbor for $3,000 plus interest.
Dottle received a $1,500 cash down payment. The real estate contract specifies that
the remaining $1,500 will be repaid at 5% interest over the next 2 years.
Analysis: The farmland is no longer Dottle's resource even though it is still their property.
Because they are bound by an agreement to sell that land, they cannot transfer title
to anyone else. Dottle has converted their ownership interest in the land into a real
estate contract. The CR determines that there is no legal restriction against converting
the contract into cash, so it is Dottle’s resource. The initial value of the contract
is the $1,500 outstanding principal balance. Counting the $1,500 principal balance
as a resource, Dottle is still under the $2,000 resource limit. Because this contract
is a resource, the principal portion of the monthly payment they receive is not income,
but the interest portion is unearned income. To determine the amount of the principal
and interest portions, the CR obtains an amortization schedule with $1,500 as the
amount of the contract, 5% interest rate, and a 24 month payment period.
2. Installment sale contract
Situation: Little, an SSI claimant, recently moved out of their home to live in a rented apartment.
They have just entered into an installment sale contract on their former home with
Higgins, an SSI beneficiary. Higgins made a $6,000 down payment on the house using
retroactive SSI benefits, and immediately moved into their new home in which they
already have an equitable ownership interest, even though they do not yet have title.
The outstanding principal balance on the installment sale agreement is $28,000.
Analysis: Resource determinations must be made for both individuals. Although Little still
has title to the house, Little cannot sell it. Its value as a resource to Little is
in the value of the installment contract. The installment sale contract (which the
CR determines has no legal restrictions against its sale) is Little's resource in
the amount of the outstanding principal balance ($28,000) unless Little presents convincing
evidence that its CMV is a lower amount. The installment sale contract has no bearing
on Higgins' eligibility, as either income or resources. Higgins' ownership interest
in the house Higgins is buying is an excluded resource since it is his principal place
of residence.
3. Reverse mortgage
Situation: Jones, an eligible individual who owns their home, enters into a mortgage contract
with a local bank. Under this contract, the bank provides them with monthly payments
which do not have to be repaid as long as they live in the home. These payments are
actually a loan against their equity in the home and must be repaid when they die,
sell the home, or move.
Analysis: Since Jones is the borrower, the mortgage contract itself is not a resource to them.
Since they live in the home, it continues to be excluded from resource counting. The
payments they receive from the reverse mortgage are loan proceeds so they are not
counted as income.
