SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 01320.450 - Deeming - Change of Status - Couples
Effective Dates: 08/05/2010 - Present | TN 60 (12-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0501320450 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Examples
1. Ineligible Spouse Becomes Eligible
In May 1988, Martha Lawton, a disabled individual, lives with their ineligible spouse
Alfred. Martha has no income and Alfred receives a monthly Social Security retirement
benefit of $310. Martha is eligible for an SSI payment in May, and the amount of that
payment is determined using deemed income from Alfred's in the budget month (March
1988). On June 1, 1988, Alfred becomes age 65 and files for SSI. Effective with June,
the deeming rules no longer apply. The Lawtons are an eligible couple. Both eligibility
and payment are determined using their combined income in June. (See SI 02005.005 for examples of computations in these cases.)
2. Ineligible Spouse and Eligible Individual Separate
On September 15, 1988, Ellen Bowers, a disabled individual, and their ineligible spouse
Thomas separate. Ellen's only income is a title II benefit of $150 per month. Thomas
works and is paid a salary of $850 per month. To determine Ellen's eligibility for
September, the couple's unearned income is reduced by the general exclusion ($150
- $20 = $130). The couple's earned income is reduced by the earned income exclusion
($850 - $65 = $785 - 1/2 remainder = $392.50). The couple's total countable income
($130 unearned + $392.50 earned = $522.50) is compared to the FBR for an eligible
couple ($532.00 in September 1988). Ellen is eligible in September. The SSI payment
amount is determined based on the couple's income in the budget month (July). Effective
with October, the deeming rules no longer apply when determining Ellen's eligibility.
However, Ellen's payment for October is determined using countable income in August,
including any deemed income from Thomas. In November, Ellen's payment amount is determined
using countable income in September, including any deemed income from Thomas. Since
the couple's countable income in September is $522.50, Ellen’s' November payment is
$9.50 ($532.00 FBR - $522.50). In December, Ellen's payment amount is determined using
only Ellen's own income received in October ($354.00 FBR - $130.00 countable income
= $224.00 SSI benefit).
3. Eligible Individual Begins Living With Ineligible Spouse
On August 2, 1988, Barbara Rogers, an ineligible spouse, returns to live with their
spouse Edward. Barbara is working and earns $700 per month. In August, Edward is eligible
for SSI based on their own unearned income of $68, and Edward's payment is determined
using Edward's countable income in June. Effective September, the deeming rules are
applied to determine eligibility. Edwards' $68 unearned income is reduced by the $20
general income exclusion, leaving $48. Barbara’s earned income is reduced by the earned
income exclusion ($65 plus one-half the remainder), leaving $317.50. The total countable
income ($365.50) is less than the FBR for a couple in September ($532), so Edward
is eligible. Edward's payment amount for September is determined based on Edward's
own countable income received in July. If Edward is eligible in October, the payment
amount is determined using Edward's countable income in August. If Edward is eligible
in November, the payment amount is determined using countable income (including deemed
income from Barbara) in September.
4. Ineligible Spouse Dies; Policy Effective December 1985
Pauline Pinot is a disabled recipient who receives $150 a month in worker's compensation.
Pauline lived with their ineligible spouse Charles until Charles died on June 15,
1988. Charles had been working part-time and received gross wages of $400 in May and
$200 in June. The FO (field office) determines that deeming no longer applies beginning
with July and redetermines Pauline's eligibility and payment amount. Effective July
1988, Pauline is eligible as an individual without a spouse and the FBR for an individual
($354 in July) applies. In determining the July payment amount, the FO uses only Pauline's
own countable income of $130 ($150 worker's compensation minus the $20 general income
exclusion). The deemed income from Charles in the budget month (May) is not
used in determining the payment amount for July. The payment amount for August is
also determined using only Pauline's own countable income in the budget month (June).
Pauline's SSI payment for July and August is $224.
5. $25 ($30) Payment Limit Applies
Michael was admitted to a nursing home on January 5, 1988, and Medicaid pays the cost
of their care. Michael's ineligible spouse is working and receives gross wages of
$900 a month. The FO determines that the $25 payment limitation applies to Michael
beginning with February 1988 and that January is the last month for which deeming
applied. Since deemed income in a prior budget month is not used to determine payment
amount once the $25 rate becomes effective for the computation month, and since Michael
has no income of their own for the budget months of December and continuing, the SSI
payment is $25 beginning with February. (If Michael remains eligible, their SSI payment
increases to $30 effective July 1, 1988, because of an increase in the payment limit.)
