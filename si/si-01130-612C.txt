SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 01130.612 - Treatment of Inherited Payments to Victims of Nazi Persecution
Effective Dates: 08/02/2024 - Present | TN 127 (10-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0501130612 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Examples
1. Intestate succession
An individual who received reparation payments died without a will. Their parents
and spouse predeceased them, and they had no children. Under state laws of intestate
succession, their sibling inherits the estate that includes $5,000 in identifiable
reparation payments. Upon submission of appropriate evidence, the funds inherited
by the sibling are excluded from resources for SSI purposes.
2. Inheritances by will
The original recipient of reparation payments from the German government died and
left $10,000 of their conserved reparation payments to their spouse in their will.
When their spouse died, they left the remaining $6,000 of conserved reparation payments
to their child, who is an SSI recipient. Upon submission of appropriate evidence,
the resource exclusion can apply to the funds inherited by the surviving child.
3. Inherited funds are not identifiable
An individual alleges they received an inheritance from their parent that included
$8,000 of Nazi reparation payments paid to their grandparent and inherited by their
parent. The individual establishes that their grandparent received payments because
of their status as a victim of Nazi persecution. However, the individual's parent
had commingled the payments with regular funds in a savings account, of which other
money had passed in and out for 20 years. The individual is unable to establish that
the funds are identifiable as reparation payments. The $8,000 is income to the individual
in the month of receipt, and any funds unspent count as a resource the following month.
