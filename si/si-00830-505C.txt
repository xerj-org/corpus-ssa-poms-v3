SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 00830.505 - Rental Income
Effective Dates: 10/15/2024 - Present | TN 257 (10-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0500830505 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Example
1. Proration of room rental expenses
Joshua Steele, an SSI recipient, rents out a room in their house to a cousin. The
house has six rooms excluding the bathroom. Since Joshua's expense (interest on a
mortgage, utilities, etc.) are for the whole house, only one-sixth of the expenses
is deducted from the gross rent.
2. Rental income interrupted
Anna Minnick, an SSI recipient, owns a multiple family residence and rents out half
of it. In 9/24, Anna's tenants leave and Anna receives no gross rent until 11/24 when
new tenants move in. Anna continues to pay mortgage and utilities on the residence
during 9/24 and 10/24. The claims specialist determines that Anna has excess expenses
and no rental income for 9/24 and 10/24 because Anna receives no gross rent for those
months. The excess expenses are carried over into the calculation of net rental income
for 11/24.
3. Gross Rent - Two owners but only one owner pays expenses
Kate Henning, an SSI recipient, owns their home jointly with Kate's son, John. Kate
rents out a couple of rooms in their house for $350/month and gives John half of it
($175/month). Kate pays all the rental expenses. To calculate Kate's net rental income,
deduct the allowable expenses Kate pays (prorated, if necessary) from $175 (Kate's
portion of the gross rent).
