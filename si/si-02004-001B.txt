B. Policy - emergency advance payments and immediate payments
We issue the EAP and IP in situations when we cannot issue regular payments timely
through Treasury certification, automated one-time payments (A-OTP), or manual one-time
payments (M-OTP) to address the individual's financial emergency. For more information
on A-OTP and M-OTP, refer to SM 01901.000. In priority of payment, we issue EAPs before
issuing IPs.
The EAP and IP are advances against an individual's future Supplemental Security Income
(SSI) payments. These advances are not additional monies due the individual. We will
recover an EAP and IP from future SSI payments for which we determine an individual
is eligible, refer to SI 02004.001B.1.d and SI 02004.001B.2.e in this section.
We may issue an EAP or IP to an individual who received interim assistance. We will
not pay the EAP or IP to the State for reimbursement. Instead, we will recover the
EAP or IP amount from SSI payments for which we determine an individual is eligible.
The payable balance is available for reimbursement to the State. For more information
on Interim Assistance Reimbursement, refer to SI 02003.001. To issue an EAP or IP, refer to SI 02004.010.
If the individual has a representative payee (payee) we must issue the EAP or IP through
the payee. For representative payee payment procedures, refer to GN 00502.001.
1. Emergency advance payment
We issue an EAP under statutory authority. We may issue a one-time EAP to certain
individuals applying for SSI. The EAP amount cannot exceed the total amount of SSI
(Federal benefit rate (FBR), plus the federally administered State supplementary payment
(SSP), if any) due the individual, in the month we issue the EAP.
a. Eligibility
An individual may receive an EAP if they will receive SSI payments based on a finding
of presumptive disability/presumptive blindness (PD/PB). If the individual is experiencing
a financial emergency and the FO cannot make a presumption of disability or blindness
determination, advise the disability determination service (DDS) of the emergency.
For PD/PB procedures, refer to DI 11055.240. The DDS may be able to make a finding of presumptive disability or blindness per
DI 23535.010.
NOTE:
We do not consider a reversed presumptive disability or blindness determination as
an overpayment, refer to SI 02201.010.
b. Initial application
We may issue an EAP to an individual even if we issued an EAP to the same individual
on a prior claim if they are filing a subsequent SSI application. We may also issue
an EAP to an individual who appealed a prior denial and was successful on appeal.
c. Financial emergency
We consider an individual having a financial emergency when they do not have sufficient
income or resources to meet an immediate threat to their health or safety, such as
the lack of food, clothing, shelter, or medical care.
An individual may have an emergency if they have liquid resources but cannot access
the resources quickly enough to meet an immediate threat to health or safety. If there
is no evidence to the contrary, accept an individual's allegation that they do not
have enough money to meet an immediate threat.
An individual has the right to choose a specific health professional, shelter, clothing,
food, etc., to meet their needs. Do not make this choice for an individual to avoid
making an EAP. For example, do not refer them to a less expensive health service of
another type or another source to avoid making an EAP.
d. Recovering an emergency advance payment
If the individual is due retroactive SSI payments, recover the full EAP amount from
the retroactive payments. If the individual is not due retroactive SSI benefits, recover
the EAP in six equal installments from the first six payments due. Recover the EAP
in fewer months if the individual makes the request, or if they are due fewer than
six months of payment, because there is a closed period of disability of less than
six months. Refer to the EAP procedures in SI 02004.010B.
2. Immediate payment
The Commissioner of Social Security (COSS) instituted the IP policy to help individuals
who do not qualify for an EAP. The IP policy applies to both Title XVI and Title II
cases, as well as concurrent cases (Title XVI eligibility and Title II entitlement).
For information about Title II IPs, refer to RS 02801.010. The maximum Title XVI IP amount is $2,000 for an individual or each member of an
eligible couple.
a. Criteria
We may make an IP when a case meets these requirements:
•
The case involves delayed payment of an initial claim, delayed or interrupted payments,
or nonreceipt of issued payments; and
•
All development is complete and eligibility for payment has been established; and
•
The individual has a financial emergency that the FO cannot resolve, or FO management
concludes that a case represents a potential public relations problem requiring prompt
action. These problems may include homelessness or a natural disaster.

b. Initial claims
In initial claims, do not make an IP without a valid Social Security number. Make
an EAP, rather than a Title XVI IP if the EAP criteria are met per SI 02004.001B.1 in this section. If a case meets the IP criteria in an initial claim, the FO may
make an IP after making an EAP.
c. Immediate payments in concurrent cases
In concurrent cases where retroactive benefits are due and windfall offset applies,
compute the windfall offset first. This computation determines the Title XVI and Title
II amounts due. For more information about Title II/Title XVI Windfall Offset, refer
to GN 02610.005.
Make the IP from the program which can pay the full IP amount. Use this chart as a
guide.

d. Posteligibility - delayed or interrupted payment
In posteligibility (PE) cases, we may make an IP if the recipient meets the criteria
upon reinstatement. We will also make an IP if payments will be delayed or stopped
due to a systems limitation or other systems problems.
e. Recovering an immediate payment
Recover the IP amount from the first regular payment following the IP.
