SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 00501.060 - Denial of SSI Benefits to Individuals Found to Have Fraudulently Misrepresented Residence in Order to Obtain Benefits Simultaneously in Two or More States
Effective Dates: 01/05/2024 - Present | TN 78 (01-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0500501060 (fetched 2026-10-04)

A. Policy
1. Ineligibility Criteria
A person is ineligible for SSI for 10 years if they were convicted in Federal or State
court of having fraudulently misrepresented residence in order to obtain simultaneously:
•
benefits or services from two or more States under programs funded under title IV
or title XIX of the Social Security Act or the Food Stamp Act of 1977, or
•
benefits in 2 or more States under the SSI program.
This provision was effective August 22, 1996 and repealed December 14, 1999. Therefore,
this policy was effective only from August 22, 1996 through December 13, 1999. The
policy is superseded by the enactment of administrative sanctions as described in
GN 02604.400.
2. Effective Date of 10-Year Period
The 10-year period begins on the date the person is convicted in Federal or State
court as described in SI 00501.060A.1. However, the first month for which an individual may be ineligible for SSI benefits
under this provision may not begin before August 1, 1996. The last month covered under
this policy is November 1999.
a. Initial Claims
The earliest possible date of SSI ineligibility is the month in which the person is
convicted, or, August 1, 1996, whichever is later. No one falls under this policy
beginning December 1, 1999.
b. Posteligibility Cases
The date of suspension is the month in which the person is convicted or, August 1,
1996, whichever is later. No date of suspension can be later than, or go beyond, November
30, 1999.
