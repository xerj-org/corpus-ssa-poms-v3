Effective Dates: 09/26/2013 - Present | TN 91 (05-24)

C. Automated wage reporting methods
SSA offers several electronic tools that SSI recipients, deemors, and representative
payees may use to report wages to SSA. Each tool has slightly different features,
but all share the following basic features:
•
accept monthly wage reports based on pay stub wage information in the reporter’s possession
from both SSI only and concurrent recipients, as well as their representative payees and deemors;
•
post the wage amount in the “Reported Amount” column on the Wages page and indicates
the amount with a “0” wage verification code on the SSR;
•
accept wage reports for only the month immediately preceding the report on any day
during the current reporting month for SSI and concurrent reporters;
•
generate a wage receipt for the SSI recipient, deemor, or their representative payee
and stores a copy of the receipt in the Online Retrieval System (ORS).
IMPORTANT: Encourage users of any of the automated wage reporting tools to report wages in the
first six days of the month to help prevent improper payments.
1. myWageReport (myWR)
Available behind the my Social Security (mySSA) portal, myWR is an automated wage reporting application for
Title II disability (SSDI), SSI, and concurrent beneficiaries.
In addition to the basic features, myWR:
•
requires the reporting individual to have a mySSA account;
•
accepts wage reports for SSDI only beneficiaries as well as SSI and concurrent recipients;
•
SSDI-only beneficiaries may report up to 24 months of past wages;
•
passes wage information to eWork when SSDI is involved;
•
displays employers for which the reporter can submit wage information based on an
EIN match either on a Wages page in the SSI claim system or in eWork;
•
accommodates recipients and deemors with multiple employers; and
•
requires input of pay stub details instead of a calculated monthly gross wage total.
IMPORTANT: Whenever possible, strongly encourage concurrent reporters to use myWR over SSAMWR
and SSITWR as myWR is designed to efficiently pass information to Title II systems.
Since the various electronic wage-reporting systems ask for information in different
formats (e.g., totaling gross wages or entering pay stub data directly), encourage
users to pick one method and continually report using that method. For additional
information on automated reporting options, see SI 00820.144.
2. SSA Mobile Wage Reporting application (SSAMWR)
The SSAMWR application allows recipients, representative payees, and deemors to report
monthly wages using a mobile device. Wage reporters can download and install the free
SSAMWR application on an Apple or Android mobile device.
In addition to the basic features, the SSAMWR system:
•
requires input of each individual pay stub instead of a calculated a monthly gross
wage total;
•
allows users the option of uploading or capturing photos of their pay stubs, which
the app reads and propagates to data fields using Optical Character Recognition and
Intelligent Document Processing technologies;
•
stores uploaded or captured photo images in the Evidence Portal (EP), which technicians
may access for wage verification purposes;
•
transmits wage data to Title II by posting to the Disability Control File (DCF). For
more information about how these monthly wage reports post to the DCF, see DI 13010.605D.2; and
•
requires the reporting individual to authenticate using their Social Security Number,
name, and date of birth.
NOTE:
Deemors reporting their own wages authenticate using their own information in the
app, and do not enter the recipient’s information. The deemor’s SSN is connected to
the recipient’s record; so, the report is posted properly.
3. SSI Telephone Wage Reporting system (SSITWR)
The SSITWR system is an automated monthly wage reporting system that allows recipients,
their representative payees, and deemors to call a toll-free number to report the
prior month’s gross wages.
In addition to the basic features, SSITWR:
•
requires wage reporters to talk into the phone to submit wages instead of keying data
on computer or smart device screens;
•
requires users to calculate their gross monthly wages and report a single dollar amount
each month;
•
allows wage reporters to include deductions, such as cafeteria plans, when trained
and directed to do so by SSA staff according to SI 00820.144C; and
•
transmits wage data to Title II by posting to the Disability Control File (DCF). For
more information about how these monthly wage reports post to the DCF, see DI 13010.605D.2.
