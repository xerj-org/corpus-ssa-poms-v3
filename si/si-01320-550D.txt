SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 01320.550 - Deeming - Change of Status - Parents/Children
Effective Dates: 10/18/2024 - Present | TN 51 (10-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0501320550 (fetched 2026-10-04)

D. Examples of deeming status changes
1. Ineligible parent becomes eligible
Gene Prescott, a disabled child, lives with their parent, Julia, and ineligible sibling.
In July, Julia is not eligible for SSI and Julia's income is deemed to Gene. However,
on August 8, Julia applies, and is determined eligible to receive SSI.
Beginning with August, Julia's income is no longer deemed to Gene to determine Gene's
eligibility. There is no deemed income from Julia in August because Julia is considered
eligible for SSI beginning in the SSI eligibility month (E02 month).
To determine the amount of Gene's SSI payment for:
•
August, use Gene's countable income in June (including any deemed income from Julia);
•
September, use Gene's countable income in July (including any deemed income from Julia);
•
October, use only Gene's countable income received in August.
2. Eligible parent becomes ineligible
In November, Julia (from the example in SI 01320.550D.1. in this section) begins receiving worker's compensation which makes Julia ineligible
to receive SSI payments.
•
In November, Gene's SSI eligibility is determined using Gene's countable income (including any
deemed income from Julia). If Gene is eligible for SSI, Gene's payment is determined
using Gene's countable income in September. There is no deemed income from Julia in
September, because Julia was eligible for SSI that month.
•
In December, Gene's SSI payment is determined using Gene's own countable income in October.
•
Beginning in January, deeming of Julia's income applies in both the eligibility and payment determinations.
(In this example, use the deeming rules in SI 01320.500).
3. Ineligible parent dies
Henry Walden is a disabled child who lives with their ineligible parents, Nicolas
and Theresa.
On June 10, 2025, Theresa Walden died. Theresa had been working and received gross
wages of $751 in April and in May, and $329 in June.
Nicolas Walden's only income is a pension check of $1,368 per month in April, May,
and June, and $1,378 per month from July on.
Henry has no income. There are no ineligible children living in the household.
In June, Henry's eligibility is determined by computing deemed income from both their parents.
•
Nicolas' unearned income of $1,368 is reduced by $20, leaving $1,348.
•
Theresa's earned income ($329) is reduced by the work expense exclusion ($65 plus
one-half the remainder) leaving $132.
•
The total income remaining ($1,480) is then reduced by the parent allocation ($1,450),
which leaves deemed income of $30.
•
The $20 general income exclusion is subtracted from Henry's income, which leaves Henry
with $10 countable income.
Because this amount is less than the Federal Benefit Rate (FBR) for an individual
for June, Henry is eligible. Henry's payment amount is determined using countable
income (including deemed income from their parents) in April.
In July, Henry's eligibility is determined using only Nicolas' income, because Henry has
no income of their own.
•
Nicolas' unearned income for July ($1,378) is reduced by $20, leaving $1,358.
•
This is then reduced by the living allowance for one parent ($967), leaving $391.
This is the amount of Nicolas' income deemed to Henry.
•
Henry's countable income is $371 ($391 deemed income minus the $20 general exclusion).
Because $371 is less than the FBR for an individual for July, Henry is eligible. Henry's
payment amount is determined based on Henry's countable income (i.e., deemed income
from Nicolas) in May.
•
In May, Nicolas received $1,368 in unearned income, which is reduced by $20, leaving
$1,348.
•
A living allowance for one parent ($967) is deducted which leaves $381 to be deemed
to Henry.
•
Henry’s deemed income is reduced by the $20 general income exclusion, leaving total
countable income of $361.
•
Henry's SSI payment for July is $606.
4. Ineligible individual adopts an eligible child
Jimmy Berlin, an eligible child, lives with Nancy Smith, who has taken care of Jimmy
for two years and files for adoption. The adoption becomes final on April 14.
•
In April, Jimmy's eligibility for SSI in April is determined based on Jimmy's own income that
month.
•
In May, Nancy's income is deemed to Jimmy to determine eligibility. If eligible, Jimmy's
SSI payment for May is determined using Jimmy's own income in the budget month (March).
•
In June, Nancy's income is deemed to Jimmy to determine eligibility and, if eligible, Jimmy's
own income in the budget month (April) is used to determine the SSI payment.
•
Beginning with July, Nancy's income is deemed to Jimmy for both the eligibility and payment determinations.
The payment amount for July is determined using Jimmy's own income and deemed income
from Nancy in the budget month (May).
5. Ineligible parents awarded joint custody of an eligible child
Mary, a 5-year old child, has been receiving SSI for 6 months. Mary lived with their
parent on April 1. (Mary's parents, Anthony and Simone separated on March 4 and filed
for a divorce). The court approved the separation agreement and granted joint custody
of the child, specifying that Mary would reside on alternate weeks with the other
parent.
On April 25, Mary went to live with Anthony in Anthony's home. On May 2, Mary returned
to Simone's home.
•
In April, deeming applies from Simone to determine Mary's eligibility for the month. If Mary
is eligible, payment is computed using income from the budget month (February). Because
Mary was still living with both parents in February, we include deemed income from
both Mary's parents.
•
In May, deeming applies from Anthony to determine Mary's eligibility, because Mary was living
with Anthony on May 1. Mary's payment is computed using income from the budget month
(March), including deemed income from both parents.
•
In June, deeming applies from Simone to determine eligibility because Mary was living with
Simone on June 1. Mary’s payment is computed using income from Simone because Mary
was living with Simone on April 1.

6. Adopted child returns to the household of a former parent
Jim is an 8-year old SSI eligible child residing in the household of their natural
parent, Mark.
In May, Jim is legally adopted by their Aunt Carol and begins living in Carol's household.
•
In June, deeming applies from Carol to determine Jim’s eligibility based on the newly established
parent-child relationship. Jim's payment is computed using income from April (including
deemed income from Mark).
•
In July, deeming applies from Carol to determine Jim’s eligibility. Jim's payment is computed
using income from May (including deemed income from Mark).
•
In August, deeming applies from Carol to determine both Jim’s eligibility and payment amount.
In October, Jim and Carol move into Mark’s household due to financial concerns. The
parent-child relationship with Mark is not re-established based on Jim’s presence
in the household, and we continue to deem from Carol. Additionally, standard living
arrangement development is undertaken to determine if Jim is receiving in-kind support
and maintenance (ISM).
7. Child is adopted by grandparents while living in the grandparent’s household with
a natural parent
Kay is an unmarried 15-year old minor child who lives in their parents’ household.
Kay gives birth to Ben, an SSI eligible child, in October.
In January, Kay’s parents legally adopt Ben. Kay and Ben continue to live in Kay's
parents’ household after Ben is adopted.
•
In February, there is no longer deeming from Kay to Ben, and deeming from Kay’s parents to Ben
begins. Ben’s eligibility is determined using Ben's countable income (including deemed
income from Kay’s parents). Ben's payment amount is computed using income from December
(including deemed income from Kay).
•
In March, Ben’s eligibility is determined using Ben's countable income (including deemed income
from Kay’s parents). Ben's payment amount is computed using income from January (including
deemed income from Kay).
•
In April, normal parent-to-child deeming rules apply. Ben’s eligibility and payment amount
are determined using Ben's countable income from February (including deemed income
from Kay’s parents).
Beginning in February, the amount of income deemed from Kay’s parents to Ben is reduced
due to an ineligible child allocation for another child in the household (Kay).
8. Ineligible parent and eligible child no longer live in the same household, but stepparent
remains with the eligible child (effective 06/16/08)
Jack is an SSI eligible 13-year-old minor child who lives with their natural parent
and the parent’s spouse, Cathy (Jack’s stepparent).
Jack’s natural parent permanently leaves the home on July 15. Jack and their stepparent,
Cathy, continue to live in the same household.
•
In August, there is no longer deeming from Jack’s parent or stepparent. Jack’s eligibility
is determined using only Jack's own income and resources. If Jack remains eligible,
Jack's payment amount is determined using Jack's income in June (including deemed
income from Jack's parent and stepparent).
•
In September, Jack’s eligibility is determined using only Jack's own income and resources. If
Jack remains eligible, Jack's payment amount is determined using Jack's income in
July (including deemed income from their parent and stepparent).
•
In October, Jack’s eligibility is determined using only Jack's own income and resources. If
Jack remains eligible, Jack's payment amount is determined using only Jack's own income
in August (no deeming from Jack’s stepparent occurs in August because Jack's parent
left the home in July).
