SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 02101.002 - Supplemental Security Income (SSI) Underpayment Due - Recipient Alive
Effective Dates: 04/03/2024 - Present | TN 38 (03-25)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0502101002 (fetched 2026-10-04)

A. Policy for releasing or withholding an underpayment
1. Paying an underpayment
If the recipient is alive, pay the underpayment to the recipient, or their representative
payee, after considering:
a. Interim assistance reimbursement (IAR) agreement
When IAR is involved, issue the first retroactive payment to a state or political
subdivision with a written authorization, rather than to the recipient. For more information
on IAR, see SI 02003.001.
b. Direct fee payment to authorized representatives
When we authorize direct fee payment to an appointed representative, pay the representative's
fee before releasing any funds to the recipient. For instructions regarding direct
fee payments, see GN 03930.060.
c. A collectible overpayment
We will impose netting to recover from an underpayment an overpayment or penalty for
which we have not waived recovery. For instructions on overpayment recovery, see SI 02220.001. For instructions on accessing penalties, see SI 02301.100. For dedicated account instructions, see SI 02101.010.
NOTE 1:
Do not recover an overpayment from funds in a dedicated account.
NOTE 2: The established recovery rate is the maximum amount that we will withhold from the
underpayment for the current calendar month. For adjustments to underpayments, see
SM 01310.410.
2. Underpayments and the No Social Security Benefits for Prisoners Act (NSSBP) provision
Do not pay a current or former recipient an underpayment if they are subject to the
NSSBP provision. A current or former recipient is subject to the NSSBP provision if
we determine they are:
•
A prisoner;
•
Confined in a public institution based on a court order for a criminal act (CPICO);
•
A fugitive felon (FF); or
•
In violation of parole or probation.
For more information on the NSSBP provision, see SI 00529.001. If the NSSBP provisions
do not apply to incarcerated recipients or former recipients due an underpayment,
follow payment instructions in GN 02607.550.
NOTE: If a recipient’s current payment status code is N02, N22, or N25, or a former recipient’s
last payment status code before termination was N02, N22, or N25, follow instructions
in SI 02101.002A.2. in this section.
3. Releasing underpayments when the recipient is also overpaid
a.
If the recipient receives an initial overpayment notice and does not appeal within
60 days or request a waiver, use any underpayment due a recipient to reduce (i.e.,
recover) any outstanding overpayment. For SSI overpayment recovery by adjustment,
see SI 02220.015.
EXAMPLE:
In October 2013, we notified John of a $2,400 overpayment occurring between June and
September 2013, due to underreported wages. We continued to post John’s wages since
John did not inform us that John was no longer working. John did not request a reconsideration
or request a waiver.
During a February 2014 redetermination, John provided proof of unemployment in October,
November, or December 2013. John was underpaid $450 between October and December 2013.
Since John did not file an appeal or request a waiver of overpayment, we applied the
$450 underpayment to reduce John's $2,400 overpayment.
b.
If the recipient requests a timely reconsideration or waiver and we have not made
a final overpayment determination, release any underpayment due by the close of the
month following the month in which we discovered the underpayment.
EXAMPLE:
In December 2013, we notified Mary of a $5,000 overpayment between March and October
2013, because Mary had more than $2,000 in countable resources. In January 2014, Mary
requested a timely reconsideration of our overpayment determination. At the same time,
Mary provided proof of resources below $2,000 starting in September. Mary was underpaid
$1,000. We released the $1,000 to Mary because we did not issue a denial of Mary’s
overpayment request until March 2014.
c.
If the recipient requests a timely reconsideration of our overpayment determination,
and we deny the reconsideration before the close of the month following the month
in which we discovered the underpayment, apply the underpayment to recover or reduce
the overpayment.
EXAMPLE:
In January, we notified Jim of a $1,500 overpayment between August and December 2013,
due to underreported wages. Jim requested a timely reconsideration of our overpayment
determination.
During the March 2014 redetermination, Jim informs us that Jim did not work in October
or November. We determine Jim has a $350 underpayment due for October through November
2013.
In April 2014, we deny the reconsideration because Jim was at fault in causing the
overpayment by not reporting wages. Since we made a final overpayment determination
before the close of the month following the month in which we discovered the underpayment,
we apply the $350 underpayment to reduce the $1,500 overpayment.
4. Releasing underpayments in installments
When we determine that an SSI recipient or couple is eligible to receive past-due
SSI payments in an amount that equals or exceeds three times the federal benefit rate
(FBR), and no exception applies, release the payments to the recipient, couple, or
their representative payee in no more than three installments, paid in six month intervals.
For instructions on releasing installment payments, see SI 02101.020.
5. Releasing underpayments into a dedicated account
If an SSI recipient who has not yet attained age 18 and who has a representative payee
is due an underpayment equal to or greater than six times the FBR, release the underpayment
into a dedicated account at a financial institution. For dedicated account instructions,
see SI 02101.010.
NOTE: Do not recover an overpayment from funds in a dedicated account.
