A. Background
Although SSI regulations equate inmate of a public institution with resident of a
public institution, the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals, in the case of Levings v.
Califano, ruled against the Social Security Administration's longstanding interpretation of
the statutory term “inmate of a public institution.” In the court's view, the word “inmate” ordinarily connotes a person confined to an institution under some form of restraint.
The court found that an individual who is voluntarily residing in an institution and
who is paying for all cost of care is not an inmate within the meaning of the statute.
SSA follows the Eighth Circuit's decision in Levings in all SSI cases involving residents of public institutions, where Medicaid is not
paying over 50 percent of the cost of care, located in the States of the Eighth Circuit.
