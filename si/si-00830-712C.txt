SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) - SI 00830.712 - Treatment of Inherited Payments to Victims of Nazi Persecution
Effective Dates: 08/02/2024 - Present | TN 260 (10-24)
Source: https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0500830712 (fetched 2026-10-04)

C. Examples
1. Intestate succession
An individual who received reparation payments died without a will. Their parents
and spouse predeceased them, and they had no children. Under state laws of intestate
succession, the individual's sibling inherits the estate that includes $5,000 in identifiable
reparation payments. Upon submission of appropriate evidence, the funds inherited
by the sibling are excluded from income for SSI purposes.
2. Series of inheritances by will
The original recipient of reparation payments from the German government died and
left $10,000 of their conserved reparation payments to their spouse in the will. When
the spouse died, the spouse left the remaining $6,000 of conserved reparation payments
to their daughter, who is an SSI recipient. Upon submission of appropriate evidence,
the income exclusion can apply to the funds inherited by the daughter.
3. Inherited funds are not identifiable
An individual alleges that they received an inheritance from their parent that included
$8,000 of Nazi reparation payments paid to their grandparent and inherited by their
parent. The individual establishes that their grandparent received payment because
of the grandparent's status as a victim of Nazi persecution. However, the individual's
parent had commingled the payments with regular funds in the parent's savings account,
of which other money had passed in and out for 20 years. The individual is unable
to establish that the funds are identifiable as reparation payments. The $8,000 is
income to the individual in the month of receipt, and any unspent funds count as a
resource the following month.
